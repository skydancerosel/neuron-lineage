Do neurons grow new jobs from old ones?

Sometimes, and more often than arbitrary directions do. In real neurons of new jobs sprout next to an earlier job, against in random directions, and the gap holds at Pythia-1.4b. The clearest links keep what a token is doing in its sentence while moving to a new kind of text, and most new jobs have no visible parent at all. Jobs a neuron drops leave little behind beyond what the text itself reveals.

The question

A neuron that fires on several unrelated things is called polysemantic. Nick Cammarata's essay Neural Networks Are Already Clean argues that most of what is left of this in modern networks is recursive generalization: a neuron's job sprouts a neighbouring job, which sprouts another, until the ends look unrelated. Its example is a neuron that goes from iy to diy to do it yourself.

The usual alternative is superposition: unrelated jobs packed into one neuron because there are more features than neurons. The two make different predictions about history. A sprout appears next to an older job of the same neuron. A packed-in job arrives with no relative. Pythia saved its weights all through training, so the history can be watched.

How it was tested

  1. Top contexts. For 512 sampled neurons per layer, and 512 random directions in the same layer as a control, the 200 places in a fixed 2M-token Pile sample where each fires most, at 11 checkpoints. At most 2 per document, with word-for-word repeats dropped.
  2. Jobs. Each context is placed in two frozen spaces: genre (what kind of text it is) and role (what the firing token is doing, read from a different model, GPT-2 medium). A job is a cluster of at least 10 contexts.
  3. New jobs. Clustering all of a unit's contexts so far, a cluster made mostly (75%) of contexts first seen at a checkpoint is a new job.
  4. Birth and parent. Rerunning those contexts through every checkpoint dates the job's birth: the first checkpoint from which they hold 20% of the unit's top-200 cutoff. Its parent is the unit's nearest job at the checkpoint just before. Jobs already active at step 128 are not tested.
  5. Lineage test. In each space separately: is the parent closer to the new job than other units' nearest jobs from the same kind of text? Closer than 95% of them counts as adjacent.

All settings were fixed before the main run, from a pilot on a different neuron sample, and written into the study doc. Planted answers checked the test's false-positive rate and power on every layer, and blind readers checked whether its links are visible.

Main result

The pre-registered test passed. In the role space, new jobs in neurons sprout beside an earlier job more often than in random directions, in every layer and most of all in layer 6.

Can blind readers see the links?

Six fresh Claude sub-agents, told nothing about labels or basis, judged 150 items. Each showed a new job's contexts beside two candidates: its real parent, and a decoy picked the same way from another unit. They had to say which one came from the same unit.

Share of items where the reader picked the real parent, with 95% intervals; 50% is chance. Source: blind_main/.

How much sprouting is there really?

The test misses many real sprouts and labels a few unrelated jobs adjacent by chance, so the observed shares need correcting. Planted answers measured both on every layer.

If a share f of new jobs truly sprout, the test reports about f × power + (1 − f) × false-positive rate. Solving for f with the best and worst controls gives a range.

Range of the true sprouting share consistent with the controls; the dot is the observed adjacent share. Assumes real sprouts sit no farther from their parents than the farthest planted ones.

Even at the test's weakest power, fewer than a third of new jobs in neurons sprout from an earlier one.

What it means for the essay

Neurons do grow partly by recursive generalization: a new job sits next to an earlier one about 4 points more often in neurons than in random directions, and twice as often per unit. The clearest cases keep a token's grammatical role and carry it into a new kind of text, a link a topic-only audit misses, though corrected for power the genre space shows lineage about as often. That fits the essay's claim that the neuron basis is special, with a different kind of evidence than its rotation search: the effect shows in how training adds jobs, not only in the finished model. Both may come from the same cause, GELU acting on each neuron separately. The same holds at Pythia-1.4b, with an advantage of about the same size.

The stronger claim, that recursive generalization explains most leftover polysemanticity, does not hold here. Even correcting for the test's misses, fewer than a third of new jobs sprout from an earlier one, and blind readers find no hidden parent for the rest.

Jobs a neuron drops do not visibly survive elsewhere. An untrained network already reads most of what the trained layer reads about them, and no SAE feature picks them out better than a neuron does. So this study finds no sign of distributed leftovers that a neuron-level audit would miss, though its SAEs are small and its ablations too blunt to settle whether the small remaining trace is used. For jobs that are still active, the best SAE feature also does no better than the best neuron, which fits the essay's own SAE comparison.

Caveats

  • Two sizes of one model family, Pythia-410m and 1.4b with plain GELU MLPs, on one corpus. The essay's strongest claims are about SwiGLU models such as Qwen3.
  • Random directions are not a pure null. They mix many neurons and inherit some of their continuity, so the gap measures what is specific to the neuron basis.
  • The same-source null runs slightly liberal (role false positives up to 7.1%); it affects both bases alike.
  • In 410m the no-change control found 0.06–0.09 spurious new jobs per unit, a little above the 0.05 the pilot used when choosing settings (1.4b: at most 0.049). Spurious jobs arise in both bases alike; the main-run rule checked only false positives.
  • Only each unit's top 200 contexts are seen, so weak or slow sprouts go undetected.
  • The blind readers were Claude sub-agents and saw only contexts: a link the model makes through circuits would not show.
  • The SAEs used for the faded-job check are small (12M training tokens, 62–68% of loss recovered), and the ablations cannot tell used from unused directions even for active jobs.

Next

Pythia-6.9b or 12b, under the same rules, would show whether the neuron advantage grows with scale; two sizes cannot. OLMo checkpoints would test SwiGLU.

Methods and files

Fixed rules

Clustering: genre semantic weight 0.5, joined-space cosine cut 0.65, new-job share 0.75, chosen by the pilot's blind planted-answer controls. Birth share 0.2, collapse share 0.25, at least 20 null units, one-sided Fisher p < 0.05 on role-space adjacency pooled over layers as the primary test.
